# LiveWeather: the SkyPulse website

A static site: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no build step. Chart.js, Leaflet and the Inter font are self-hosted in `vendor/` (unmodified npm releases with their licences), so the page loads no third-party code. GitHub Pages serves this folder as-is. See the [repository README](../README.md) for an overview and deployment.

## How it works

### Location
1. `navigator.geolocation.watchPosition` (high accuracy) starts on load.
2. The first fix loads data. Later fixes only update the accuracy badge, unless you have moved at least `MOVE_KM` (1.5 km). A move triggers a full refetch and a feed entry.
3. If permission is denied or there is no fix:
   - it uses the last place saved in `localStorage` (`skypulse.lastLoc`);
   - otherwise it shows a gate offering a GPS retry or city search.
4. Choosing a searched city switches to manual mode. The **Live GPS** button resumes tracking.

### Data
Three requests run in parallel on every network refresh. Air quality and reverse geocoding are optional, so their failures are ignored. Coordinates sent to any third party are rounded to 3 decimals (~110 m).

| Request | Endpoint | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Forecast | `api.open-meteo.com/v1/forecast` | `timezone=auto`, `timeformat=unixtime`, `past_days=2`, `forecast_days=8`. Returns `current`, 18 `hourly` variables and `daily`. `minutely_15` has `forecast_minutely_15=96`, `past_minutely_15=4`: all 12 variables in the US/Europe, precipitation elsewhere. |
| Air quality | `air-quality-api.open-meteo.com/v1/air-quality` | `us_aqi`, `pm2_5`, `pm10` |
| Place name | `api.bigdatacloud.net/data/reverse-geocode-client` | Only for GPS fixes without a saved name; searched cities already have names |

- **15-minute data (US & Europe):** Open-Meteo serves 15-minute forecasts free on its non-commercial API, from native 15-minute models over North America and central Europe; elsewhere it interpolates hourly data.
  - **When:** used when the place's country is the US or a European country, or, before the country is known, when the point is inside a rough US/Europe box. Settings → *15-minute data* can turn it off.
  - **Variables:** temperature, feels like, humidity, precipitation, rain, snowfall, weather code, wind, direction, gusts, visibility, day/night.
  - **Used by:** the *Next 6 hours · 15-minute steps* card (chart + plain-language summary), the Pulse Advisor (precipitation rate, gusts and feels-like in the window) and the nowcast tile.
  - **Fallback:** if Open-Meteo ever rejects the step parameters (HTTP 400), the app repeats the request in its previous form.
- **Refresh cadence:** Open-Meteo updates current conditions every 15 minutes, so the weather refreshes at a quarter-hour mark plus 1 min, nearest to one cache TTL (default 15 min) after the last fetch. Before this change it was every 5 min.
  - Warnings and the cyclone feed are checked every 5 min on their own timer (`HZ_MS`), so NWS warnings stay as timely as before.
  - When the tab becomes visible again it refreshes if due; on failure it retries after 1 min.
- **On-device cache (`cache.js`):**
  - **What is kept:** each network fetch saves the raw forecast, air quality and place name under its own `localStorage` key, plus an index. At most 8 places are kept, and entries older than 24 h are purged.
  - **When it is used:** opening the app, picking a searched place or returning to the tab within the TTL (Settings: 15/20/30 min or off) uses the copy within 1.5 km instead of a new request; the status line shows "saved copy".
  - **Manual refresh** (button, pull-to-refresh) always fetches.
  - **Offline:** if the network fails and nothing is loaded, the newest copy is shown, however old, labelled "offline copy", with a retry every minute.
  - **Other caches:** GPS fixes reuse saved place names, so the reverse geocoder is asked once per place. The coastline probe is cached per spot (`skypulse.coast`, 12 spots). Search results are memoised for the session.
- **Time handling:** all timestamps are Unix epochs and are shown in the *location's* time zone via `Intl.DateTimeFormat`, in the chosen 12/24-hour style, so DST is handled correctly.
- **Daily rows:** daily timestamps are local midnight, so they are labelled after re-applying `utc_offset_seconds`.
- **Stale responses:** a fetch token discards responses that belong to an older location.

### Pulse Advisor (`pulse.js`, rendered by `renderPulse`)
The successor to the umbrella advisor. It looks at every hour, and every 15-minute slot where available, from now to now + window (1/3/6/12/24 h).

**How it decides.**
- Each factor proposes advice on one scale: **0 clear · 1 low · 2 moderate · 3 caution · 4 high · 5 extreme**.
- The highest level wins. On a tie, the more specific hazard wins (cyclone > official warning > wind > ice > hail > thunder > downpour > snow > heat/cold > fog > air > UV).
- The other factors at level ≥ 2 become "Also:" notes.
- When an official warning ties with a specific hazard, the headline gives the specific advice and the detail cites the warning.

| Factor | Thresholds (standard profile) | Advice |
|---|---|---|
| Rain need (the old umbrella logic, rain threshold `T`) | 0 / 1 (peak ≥ 0.6·T or ≥ 0.2 mm) / 2 (peak ≥ T, ≥ 1 mm, nowcast ≥ 0.5 mm, or raining now) | No umbrella needed · Pack a compact umbrella · Take an umbrella / Umbrella needed now |
| Rain + gusts ≥ 60 km/h | level 1–2 | Rain jacket, not an umbrella |
| Precipitation rate (hourly, or 15-min × 4) | ≥ 7.6 / 20 / 50 mm/h → 2 / 3 / 4 | Heavy rain · Downpours — limit time outside · Torrential rain — avoid going out |
| Thunder / hail / freezing rain | 2–3 / 4 / 4 | Stay near shelter · Stay under cover · Icy, avoid going out |
| Snow (light / heavy / with gusts ≥ 50) | 2 / 3 / 4 | Hooded jacket · Avoid travel · Blowing snow |
| Gusts | ≥ 40 / 62 / 75 / 89 / 118 km/h → 1–5 | Strong gusts … Stay indoors — destructive winds |
| Cyclone wind at your point (`Hazards`) | ≥ 25 kt → 3, ≥ 34 kt → 5 | Cyclone nearby · Stay indoors — cyclone winds |
| Feels-like heat | ≥ 32 / 36 / 40 / 45 / 52 °C → 1–5 | Stay hydrated … Dangerous heat |
| Feels-like cold | ≤ 0 / −10 / −20 / −28 / −35 °C → 1–5 | Dress in layers … Dangerous cold — stay indoors |
| Visibility | < 1000 m → 1, < 200 m → 3 | Mist · Dense fog |
| US AQI / UV | ≥ 101/151/201/301 → 1–4; UV ≥ 8 → 1, ≥ 11 → 2 | Limit exertion / mask · Sunscreen |
| Warnings overlapping the window | official red / orange / yellow → 5 / 4 / 2; SkyPulse flood, surf, cyclone or surge red / orange / yellow → 4 / 3 / 1 | Follow official advice |

- **Profiles:** *Sensitive* lowers the heat thresholds by 3 °C, raises the cold ones by 5 °C, scales gusts by 0.85 and adds a level for poor air. *Hardy* goes the other way.
- **The card shows:**
  - a six-segment level ring, the verdict and its reason;
  - a "what to take" list (umbrella or windproof umbrella, rain jacket, waterproof shoes, layers, water, sunscreen, mask, lights, shelter); sun gear is dropped when the advice is to stay in or it pours;
  - six factor meters (rain & snow, wind, feels like, visibility, air & UV, alerts);
  - *Advisories & warnings*: the SkyPulse and official warnings in the window, the cyclone threat near you, and a link to your national weather service (`Hazards.localAgency`: NWS for the point, about 80 national services, MeteoAlarm for the rest of Europe);
  - the hourly rain-chance bars with the threshold slider.

### Sun tracker (`sun.js` + `buildSunTracker` / `updateSunTracker`)
Everything is calculated in the browser from latitude, longitude and time; no API is involved. It uses the NOAA solar calculator equations (after Meeus), which are accurate to about 1 minute.

1. **Time of year → `SunCalc.solar(t)`**. From the Julian century it derives the sun's mean longitude and anomaly, orbital eccentricity, apparent longitude and obliquity. These give the **declination** δ (how far north or south the sun is overhead) and the **equation of time** (how far sundials run ahead of or behind clocks).
2. **Live position → `SunCalc.position(t, lat, lon)`**.
   - True solar time = UTC minutes + equation of time + 4·longitude; the hour angle follows from it.
   - Elevation: `sin h = sin φ sin δ + cos φ cos δ cos H`, plus a standard atmospheric-refraction correction.
   - Azimuth is measured clockwise from north.
3. **Events for the local date → `SunCalc.times(utcMidnight, lat, lon, utcOffset)`**.
   - Solar noon = 720 − 4·lon − EoT minutes. The UTC offset makes sure it is the noon belonging to the *local* calendar day, which matters near the date line.
   - Each event solves `cos H = (sin h₀ − sin φ sin δ)/(cos φ cos δ)`, re-evaluating δ at the event time (4 iterations).
   - Thresholds h₀:
     - sunrise/sunset: −0.833° (refraction + solar radius)
     - civil, nautical and astronomical twilight: −6°, −12°, −18°
     - golden hour: +6°
   - If |cos H| > 1, the result is reported as **polar day** or **polar night**.

What the card shows (it is the last section on the page, since weather comes first):
- **Elevation curve:** spans sunrise to sunset; for polar days and nights it spans local midnight to midnight.
  - Golden hours are shaded. The path already travelled is drawn solid and the rest dashed.
  - A strip below the horizon (down to −18°) shows how deep the sun is at night.
  - **Hover or drag** anywhere on it for a tooltip giving the time (with "in 2h 10m" or "3h ago"), sun angle, azimuth, shadow length and light phase. The hovered position is mirrored as a white dot on the sky dome.
- **Sky dome:** a top-down polar plot (zenith in the centre, horizon at the rim) with the day's path, the sunrise and sunset azimuths, and the live sun.
- **Stats (12):**
  - updated every second: elevation and phase, azimuth, shadow length (1/tan h), and a countdown to sunset or sunrise;
  - fixed for the day:
    - sunrise and sunset (time, azimuth, civil dawn/dusk);
    - solar noon (direction of the sun, offset from 12:00 on the clock);
    - **max sun angle today** (vs the year's peak and tomorrow);
    - **max sun angle this year** (peak date, number of overhead/90° days in the tropics, lowest noon sun);
    - day length with the change vs yesterday and tomorrow;
    - golden hours and astronomical twilight.
- **Year chart:**
  - Shows day length (left axis, 0–24 h) and the noon sun angle (right axis, 0–90°) for every day of the current year.
  - Marks today and the extremes of both series, and gives midnight-sun or polar-night day counts at high latitudes.
  - **Hover or tap** any day to see its date (with "in N days" or "N days ago"), day length versus today, max sun angle with the solar-noon time, and sunrise and sunset.
  - Daily values are computed once per place and year by `yearData()` (366 × `SunCalc.times`) and cached.
  - **Tropics:** zenith-passage days are found where the solar declination crosses your latitude, and every one gets a marker at 90°. Noon sampling can miss the exact crossing by up to ~0.2°, so those days are pinned to 90°.
  - **Polar edges:** on the first and last day of the midnight sun or polar night, the sunrise/sunset solver can converge on only one side. Day length is then integrated numerically (5-min steps), and the curve covers midnight to midnight with only the rise or set that exists.

When the static parts are rebuilt:
- they are rebuilt when the location's calendar date, the location or the window size changes;
- the SVGs are drawn at real pixel width so text stays readable on phones;
- the tracker uses the coordinates of the *loaded* dataset, so it never mixes a new location with the previous location's time zone.

### Tropical cyclones (`hazards.js`)

**Data.**
- `pipeline/fetch_tropical.py` runs on every deploy and every 30 minutes. It writes `data/tropical.json`, which the site loads from its own origin, because the agencies' feeds don't send CORS headers.
- One record is built per storm. The official regional centre is primary where its data can be parsed:
  - NHC for AL/EP/CP;
  - JMA for the West Pacific;
  - otherwise JTWC.

  JTWC is attached as `alt`, which fills in quadrant radii, past track and maximum wave height.
- Extra sources:
  - IMD's official cone and 28/34/50/64-kt wind areas are attached to North Indian Ocean storms;
  - GDACS adds the alert level, affected countries and the model storm-surge and rainfall maps.

| Agency | Parsed from | Fields |
|---|---|---|
| NHC | `CurrentStorms.json`, forecast advisory (TCM), public advisory (TCP), cone KMZ, best-track KMZ | position, 1-min wind, gusts, pressure, eye, 34/50/64-kt and 12-ft/4-m sea radii by quadrant, forecast to 120 h, headline, watches/warnings, hazards (wind, rain, surge, surf) |
| JMA | bosai `targetTc.json`, `specifications.json`, `forecast.json` | 10-min wind, gusts, pressure, category/intensity/size, gale (30 kt) and storm (50 kt) radii, 70 % probability circles, track history |
| JTWC | RSS → `*.tcw` + warning text | 1-min wind, gusts, quadrant radii, forecasts, past track, pressure, maximum significant wave height, remarks |
| IMD | `dss.imd.gov.in/dwr_img/GIS/tout*.geojson` | official cone and wind-area polygons (empty when no storm is active) |
| GDACS | event list + event details | alert level, countries, rain and surge map images |

**Threat analysis (per storm, hourly from now to the last forecast).**
1. **Track interpolation:**
   - position, intensity and quadrant radii are interpolated linearly between forecast points;
   - longitudes are unwrapped across the dateline;
   - JMA 10-min winds are converted to the 1-min equivalent (÷ 0.88).
2. **Wind at your point:** a piecewise power-law vortex passes through the agency radii: RMW → R64 → R50 → R34, then decays as r⁻⁰·⁶.
   - Radii are blended smoothly between quadrant centres.
   - RMW is estimated from R64, R50 or R34.
   - Gusts use the agency's gust factor (default 1.25).
3. **Wind direction:** cyclonic flow (anticlockwise in the north, clockwise in the south) plus ~20° inflow. Wind comes *from* the bearing from the centre to you, + 70° in the northern hemisphere and − 70° in the southern.
4. **Closest approach and side of track:** the cross product with the storm's heading gives the side you're on. The right side in the north (left in the south) is labelled *dangerous*.
5. **Threat level:**
   - **direct**: gale-force winds are estimated at your point, or the centre passes within 150 km;
   - **near**: within 500 km;
   - **watch**: within 1000 km and approaching.
6. **Coastline probe (Open-Meteo Elevation, 81 points):**
   - points are sampled on rings at 1.5, 4, 8, 15 and 30 km in 16 directions;
   - DEM cells exactly 0 m are open sea;
   - from these it derives the distance to the sea, the seaward bearing (weighted circular mean) and your ground elevation.
7. **Onshore / offshore:** wind from within 60° of the seaward bearing is *onshore*, wind from within 60° of the opposite direction is *offshore*, anything else is *alongshore*.
8. **Storm-surge exposure** is scored from:
   - peak onshore wind (34/50/64 kt);
   - being on the dangerous side;
   - ground elevation (< 5 m raises the score, > 15 m lowers it);
   - distance from the coast.

   It's reported as low, moderate, elevated or high, and NHC's official surge statement is quoted when one exists.
9. **Rain and waves:**
   - model rainfall over the closest approach ± 24 h, with NHC's rainfall statement quoted;
   - Open-Meteo Marine at the nearest sea point: current and peak wave height, period, direction and sea temperature;
   - the agency's maximum wave height and sea radii.

**Cyclone tracker (`storms.js`, the Storms tab):** one Leaflet map (Esri dark canvas tiles) with **every** active storm.
- **Per storm:**
  - the cone: the official NHC or IMD one if published, otherwise convex hulls of NHC-average error circles (JMA's own probability circles for JMA storms);
  - the past track coloured by intensity;
  - the forecast track with Saffir–Simpson-coloured points;
  - the current 34/50/64-kt quadrant radii;
  - a rotating cyclone symbol (mirrored in the southern hemisphere) and a label.
- **Layers:** each one switches on or off, and the choice is remembered.
- **One continuous strip of map:** storms are unwrapped around a reference longitude chosen to minimise the span, so dateline storms and the Atlantic stay together.
- **Selecting a storm** (list, symbol or track point) zooms to it. It adds IMD wind areas, JMA probability circles and your line of closest approach, and fills the detail card below: threat, facts, local scales, impacts, forecast table, bulletins and links.
- **Intensity forecast:** an SVG chart of 1-minute winds (JMA 10-minute winds ÷ 0.88) over Saffir–Simpson bands, with the observed past, a now line and hover details. It shades a **typical-error band** (approx. ±6 kt at 12 h to ±16 kt at 120 h), labelled as a guide, not an official product.
- **Forecast slider and play:** moves every storm along its interpolated forecast (with wind radii for the selected storm).
- **Full screen:** the ⛶ button opens the map full screen (Esc closes it); the 🌐 button zooms out to all storms.
- **Wheel zoom** only works once the map has focus, so the page still scrolls past it.
- **Defensive:** every storm from `tropical.json` is sanitised on load, so numbers are numbers and rings are numeric pairs. Links and images must be `http(s)`.

**Local scales:**
- Saffir–Simpson;
- JMA (10-min);
- IMD (3-min);
- Météo-France SW Indian Ocean;
- Australian/Fiji categories.

Each storm also links to your national service (about 50 countries, matched by country code).

### Warnings (`hazards.js`)
- **Official (live in the browser):**
  - NWS `alerts/active?point=` for US states and territories;
  - Hong Kong Observatory `warnsum`.
- **Official (from the pipeline):** India's NDMA SACHET CAP alerts (IMD and the state SDMAs). Each alert is matched to your **district or city** using its area and headline text; otherwise it's matched to your **state** (shown as "in your state").
- **Official cyclone areas:**
  - inside the NHC or IMD cone;
  - inside IMD's 28/34/50/64-kt areas;
  - inside JMA's gale or storm area now, or in a forecast storm-warning area;
  - a GDACS alert for your country.
- **SkyPulse forecast hazards (next 48–72 h):**

  | Hazard | Yellow / orange / red |
  |---|---|
  | Heavy rain (24 h) | IMD thresholds 64.5 / 115.6 / 204.5 mm, or bursts ≥ 30 mm/h |
  | Gusts | ≥ 62 / 89 / 118 km/h (the cyclone estimate counts) |
  | Waves at the nearest sea | ≥ 2.5 / 4 / 6 m |
  | Heat (feels like) | ≥ 40 / 45 / 52 °C |
  | Wind chill | ≤ −20 / −28 / −35 °C |

  Also covered: thunderstorms (orange when hail is forecast), dense fog, freezing rain, and cyclone wind and surge warnings.
- **Display:**
  - official warnings are sorted first within each level;
  - expired official warnings are dropped;
  - the count appears on the Alerts tab badge;
  - each new orange or red warning (and every official one) is logged once in the live feed.

### Weather effects (`fx.js`)
`FX.set({ kind, intensity, hail, windKmh, windFromDeg, hemisphere, stormBearing })` drives one full-screen canvas behind the glass cards.
- **Drops:** each drop has a depth (0–1) that sets its length, speed, thickness and opacity. Drops are batched into three depth layers, one path each.
- **Wind and gusts:** wind slants the rain, and random gusts vary the slant and the curtains of rain.
- **Scenes:**
  - *drizzle*: short, thin, slow drops plus drifting mist and fog;
  - *rain*: light or moderate;
  - *heavy*: long dense streaks, splashes where drops land, sweeping rain curtains, darker sky;
  - *storm*: heavy rain with branching lightning (double-pulse flash) and distant sheet lightning, plus bouncing hail for codes 96/99;
  - *cyclone*: near-horizontal wind-driven rain, spray streaks, tumbling debris, and spiral rain bands rotating anticlockwise in the north and clockwise in the south, centred towards the storm's real bearing;
  - *snow*: flakes with sway and depth;
  - *fog*, *clouds* and *stars*.
- **Choosing a scene:** it is picked from the current WMO code and the precipitation rate. *cyclone* takes over when a direct threat brings gale-force winds within 3 h.
- **Performance and accessibility:**
  - `prefers-reduced-motion` draws a single static frame;
  - the loop pauses while the tab is hidden;
  - phones get roughly 55 % of the particles at ≤ 1.5× device pixel ratio.

### Phone app shell and PWA
- **Tabs (≤ 760 px wide):** each section carries `data-tab="now|forecast|alerts|storms|sun|feed"`, and `main[data-active]` shows one screen at a time. A tab whose sections are all hidden in Settings disappears from the bar.
- **Navigation:**
  - bottom tab bar with an alert count and a feed dot;
  - horizontal swipe between tabs (ignored on charts, maps and scrollers);
  - pull-to-refresh;
  - search opens as a sheet;
  - `?tab=` deep links, also used by the manifest shortcuts.
- **Re-layout:** components measured while hidden (chart, sun tracker, map) re-layout when their tab is shown.
- **PWA:**
  - `manifest.webmanifest` with icons (`icons/`, generated by `pipeline/make_icons.py`) and Android shortcuts;
  - the *Install* button uses `beforeinstallprompt`;
  - `sw.js` (HTTPS only) fetches its own files (including `vendor/`) network-first with an offline fallback. It stores only successful same-origin responses and never touches the weather APIs or map tiles.
- **Assets:** loaded with `?v=N`. Bump N and `VERSION` in `sw.js` on each release.

### Live feed
- **After each refresh:** the current conditions are compared with the previous snapshot. Thresholds: temperature ±0.5 °C, humidity ±4 %, cloud ±15 %, pressure ±0.8 hPa, wind ±6 km/h, or a change of weather code. If nothing changed, it logs "steady".
- **Pulse Advisor changes:** a change of level is logged only if the window, threshold and profile are unchanged.
- **Memory:** at most `FEED_MAX` entries are kept; announcement keys older than 48 h are forgotten, so a PWA left open for days doesn't grow; only new entries animate.
- **Upcoming events:** checked every refresh and every 30 s, and announced once each:
  - rain ≥ T within 3 h;
  - nowcast rain within 60 min;
  - thunder within 6 h;
  - sunrise/sunset within 1 h;
  - UV ≥ 6;
  - gusts ≥ 60 km/h.

### Settings (`settings.js`)
The settings button (sliders icon) in the top bar opens a sheet (keyboard: focus is trapped, Esc closes it). Every value is validated against a fixed list on load, so a damaged or edited `localStorage` falls back to defaults. Old `unit` / `umbWin` / `umbThr` keys are carried over once.

| Group | Options |
|---|---|
| Units | temperature °C/°F; wind km/h, mph, m/s, knots; precipitation mm/in; pressure hPa/inHg/mmHg; distance km/mi (also storm distances and wave heights); clock auto/12 h/24 h. The top-bar °C/°F switch applies the metric or imperial set. |
| Appearance | theme (live sky / night / black OLED / high contrast), six accent colours, comfortable/compact density, weather effects full/lite/off |
| Layout | show, hide and reorder the 12 sections; show or hide each detail tile; start tab on phones; reset |
| Pulse Advisor | profile (standard / sensitive / hardy), look-ahead window, rain threshold |
| Data & privacy | 15-minute data auto/off; cache 15/20/30 min/off; how much is saved; *Clear saved data*; reset everything |

**Layout engine (`Layout.apply`):**
- It packs the visible sections into rows of 12 columns in the user's order, with desktop spans of hero 7, Pulse 5, chart 8, feed 4, daily 7, past 5; the rest are full width.
- The Pulse card spans two rows, beside both the hero and the warnings.
- The last item of a short row stretches.
- A MutationObserver re-packs when a section shows or hides itself (e.g. the 15-minute card outside the US/Europe).
- Phones keep the hand-tuned per-tab order until the user reorders.

### Persistence
Everything is in `localStorage`, wrapped in try/catch, and never uploaded:
- `skypulse.settings`: preferences;
- `skypulse.tab`: the last tab;
- `skypulse.lastLoc`: the last place;
- `skypulse.wxc.*`: the weather cache;
- `skypulse.coast`: coastline probes.

*Settings → Clear saved data* removes the cache, coastline probes and last place.

### Security & privacy
- **Content-Security-Policy** (meta tag):
  - scripts only from the site itself;
  - `connect-src` limited to the listed weather, alert and place-name APIs;
  - images limited to the map tiles and GDACS;
  - no plugins, `base-uri 'self'`, no forms.
- **No third-party code or fonts:** Chart.js, Leaflet and Inter are self-hosted. This removes CDN supply-chain risk and the IP disclosure to CDNs and Google Fonts.
- **Untrusted text:** every feed string is HTML-escaped. Links and images from feeds pass `safeUrl()` (`http`/`https` only). Storm records are coerced to numbers before maths or markup. The smoke test carries XSS probes to keep it that way.
- **Requests:** API calls use `credentials: 'omit'`. Third-party coordinates are rounded to ~110 m. There are no cookies, analytics or accounts.

## Tuning
Constants at the top of `app.js`:

```js
const QH = 15 * 60e3;          // Open-Meteo's update cycle; refreshes align to it (+1 min)
const HZ_MS = 5 * 60e3;        // warnings / cyclone feed polling
const MOVE_KM = 1.5;           // refetch after moving this far
const FEED_MAX = 60;           // feed entries kept
```

The cache keeps `MAX = 8` places for `KEEP_MS = 24 h` (`cache.js`). Pulse thresholds are at the top of each factor in `pulse.js`.

## Testing tips
- `node ../tests/pulse.test.mjs` checks the Pulse Advisor logic. `../tests/smoke.mjs` drives the whole site in Chromium against mocked APIs; see `../tests/README.md`.
- Geolocation needs HTTPS or `localhost`. Serve the folder with `py -m http.server 8090` from inside `LiveWeather/`, or with `--directory LiveWeather` from the repo root.

## Known limitations
- 15-minute data is native only where Open-Meteo has 15-minute models (North America, central Europe). Elsewhere in Europe, and in the nowcast outside the US/Europe, it is interpolated from hourly data.
- Official warnings are ingested for the US (NWS), India (SACHET), Hong Kong and cyclone areas. Elsewhere the Pulse Advisor links to the national service (or MeteoAlarm in Europe) instead of listing its warnings.
- The intensity error band is an approximate climatology of official forecast errors, not a probabilistic forecast for the storm.
- If the air-quality API is unavailable, that tile is omitted.
- Hourly rain probability is per hour. The advisor uses the peak hour rather than a combined "any rain" probability, which would overstate correlated hours.
- Cyclone impacts at your point (wind, onshore/offshore timing, surge exposure) are **estimates** from agency radii and a simple vortex model. They aren't an official storm-surge forecast; use the official text quoted in the card.
- IMD publishes machine-readable products only as cone and wind-area polygons. Intensity and track for North Indian Ocean storms therefore come from JTWC, with IMD's scale estimated from 1-min winds (× 0.95).
- SACHET's alert polygons are not publicly downloadable, so India alerts are matched by state and district names in the CAP text.
- Data sources that fail during a pipeline run are recorded in `tropical.json → status`, and the site keeps working with whatever succeeded.
